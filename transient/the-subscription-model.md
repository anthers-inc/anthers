# The Subscription Model   
### The Subscription Pool   
Bluebell's primary funding mechanism is a **subscription pool**. Users pay a monthly subscription to the network (not to individual creators), and that subscription is distributed automatically to the creators they consume.   
This solves the biggest friction point in subscription models: **decision fatigue**. Asking someone to individually evaluate and subscribe to fifteen different creators is exhausting. Most people would rather put $20/month into a pool and have it flow naturally to the people they actually spend time with.   
### Baseline + Discretionary Allocation   
The pool distribution operates on two layers:   
**Baseline (automatic).** By default, a user's subscription is distributed proportionally based on consumption time — how much of each creator's content they actually watched. This requires no user action and produces a fair outcome for passive users, which will be the majority. A **stepping function** enforces minimum thresholds: if you consume a meaningful proportion of your time from one creator, they receive at least X% of your subscription. This protects creators from having their baseline yanked away and prevents gaming.   
**Discretionary (manual).** On top of the baseline, users have an allowance of custom allocation they can adjust. This allows them to express *value* rather than just *attention* — boosting a small creator they love over a background music channel they watch more of. Critically, this layer is **additive**, not redistributive. Users never feel like they are taking from one creator to give to another. The baseline is the baseline. The discretionary layer is additional appreciation.   
This two-layer model has a beneficial psychological property: the default behavior of just watching stuff and not touching any settings still produces a fair outcome. The discretionary layer is for people who care enough to express preference, and it generates a signal that does not exist on any current platform — a value signal distinct from an attention signal.   
### Discovery Through Value Alignment   
The discretionary allocation data, in aggregate and anonymized, creates a novel recommendation signal. Imagine a feed that surfaces creators you do not currently subscribe to, but that people with similar funding patterns support. That is discovery driven by **value alignment** rather than engagement metrics — qualitatively different from watch-time or click-through rate optimization.   
### No Ads, Ever   
Bluebell does not serve ads. This is not just a policy preference — it is a structural commitment enforced by the organizational model. The platform's revenue comes from a share of subscription pool throughput (kept as small as possible to sustain infrastructure and development) and potentially from the managed hosting service for creator nodes. There is no ad inventory to sell, no engagement metric to optimize for advertisers, and therefore no incentive to hijack the user experience.
